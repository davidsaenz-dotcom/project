Newtonian Gravitation

What is Newtonian Gravitation?

Newtonian Gravitation is a formula that helps us get the magnitude of the force of gravity between two objects. The core relationship of the formula is that when the distance between the two objects increases, the magnitude of the gravitational force between the objects is reduced, and that if either of the masses is increased, so will the magnitude of the force of gravity between the two objects.

What is Gravitation Dependent On?

It is dependent on 3 different variables and the universal gravitation constant G:

  • m1: This is the mass of the first object, and is measured in kilograms.
  • m2: This is the mass of the second object, and is also measured in kilograms.
  • r: This is the radius of the gravitational trajectory of the satellite, or in other words, the distance between the centers of mass of the two objects. It is measured in meters.
  • G: This is the universal constant of gravitation. It is equal to 6.67430(15) x 10-11 m3kg-1s-2 or Nm2/kg2
  • F or Fg: This is the magnitude of the force of gravity between both objects.
